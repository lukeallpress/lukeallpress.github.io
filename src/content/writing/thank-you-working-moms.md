Celebrating working moms today — the ones I'm related to and the ones I work with who are leading, problem-solving, mentoring, organizing, and showing up all day, then going home and doing household work that still falls disproportionately on them.

The numbers aren't subtle. Globally, women perform roughly three-quarters of all unpaid care work. In the workplace, motherhood still gets treated as a risk factor in ways fatherhood usually doesn't — and that bias shows up in hiring, promotions, flexibility, and quiet assumptions about ambition.

I've learned many of my most important colleague and manager skills from being a parent. Moms are so often cast as the primary parent that they build deep reserves of emotional and social skill just to hold it all together — plus the sheer grit of having grown whole humans, in their own bodies or gathered through other means. I'm grateful to work alongside so many of them.

Working moms raise feminist sons. Thanks, Mom.

And a shoutout to my partner, who runs a school all day and then comes home and somehow finds the energy to paint, puzzle, plan adventures, and raise three amazing (and wild) kids with me.
