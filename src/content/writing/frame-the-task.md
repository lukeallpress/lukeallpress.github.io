A colleague pushed my thinking recently with the idea of a *micro-committee*, and it clicked against something I'd been noticing in my own work.

Small group. Clear task. Short timeline. Right people.

It feels like a leadership version of something I keep running into with AI agents and vibe coding. The real skill isn't "prompting" for me anymore — it's framing. Coding with agents makes you feel more like an architect than a construction worker. I think *really* hard for ten minutes to set up the project, and then mostly I monitor progress toward the goal.

But it only works well when the task is framed well. What is the actual objective? What are the boundaries? What context matters? What does "done" look like?

That's true for a micro-committee, too. My understanding of leadership is increasingly about setting the work up clearly enough that others can move with confidence.

> Frame the task. Name the guardrails. Define success.

Then trust the team — human or digital — to work inside the bounds.
