Good guidance doesn't help anyone if no one reads it. The 2026 Arizona Generative AI guidance is a thorough, 41-page document — exactly the kind of thing busy educators skim once and shelve.

So I built a companion to it: an interactive reading guide that meets people where they are. Instead of asking everyone to read cover to cover, it offers **role-based reading routes** — a superintendent, a classroom teacher, and a CTO each get a tailored path through the pages that matter most to them.

It also makes the document easier to live with: a **"What changed in 2026"** view that flags new and expanded sections, themed filters for understanding the revisions, expandable section summaries, direct page links, and shareable, role-specific announcements districts can drop into their own communications.

It's a small but pointed example of the work I care about — taking serious policy and making it genuinely usable, with AI literacy framed as something every role can act on.
